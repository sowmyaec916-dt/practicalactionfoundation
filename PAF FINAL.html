Why we exist

Hard problems rarely sit inside one sector.

Extreme heat is a health, water, housing and livelihood problem at once. Food loss is about roads, storage, markets and credit as much as farming. Lasting outcomes come from combining the pieces, and from someone taking responsibility for making them work together on the ground.

  1. PeopleCommunities and frontline workers define the problem and test the answer.
  2. InstitutionsMunicipalities, district bodies, collectives and local organisations carry delivery and keep it going.
  3. TechnologyFrom simple, appropriate tools to digital and frontier tools, chosen for use, not novelty.
  4. EnterpriseLivelihoods, producer businesses and markets that keep working after a project closes.
  5. FinancePhilanthropic, CSR, public and private capital, sequenced to prove, de-risk and then grow.
  6. EvidenceBaselines, measurement and learning that improve delivery while it is running.
  1. i

    Start with the ground reality

    Listen to people, institutions and markets before designing anything.

  2. ii

    Prove it practically

    Test in real settings and build evidence on adoption, inclusion and economics.

  3. iii

    Build for continuity

    Connect finance, enterprise, skills, data and local ownership from the start.

  4. iv

    Hand over well

    Help partners and local institutions carry proven approaches forward on their own.

Theme 01

Food Systems

Why this matters

Farmers carry the risk of every weather shock and every price swing. Produce loses value between the field and the buyer, through handling, storage and weak market links, and those losses are rarely measured. Producer institutions often lack the capital and systems to act together. We work across the whole chain, so more of what is grown is sold, and more of the value stays with the people who grow it.

Climate Resilience

Why this matters

Heat, floods, drought and storms reach households first, and they hit hardest where housing, water and incomes are already fragile. Risk is rarely one hazard. It is the combination of exposure, vulnerability and weak local systems. We help communities and local institutions understand that risk, act on it early, and restore the ecosystems that protect them.

Theme 03

Energy and WASH

Why this matters

Clean water, safe sanitation and reliable energy are the services every other outcome depends on: health, learning, livelihoods and dignity. The technology often exists. What fails is the service chain around it: who runs it, who pays for it, and who fixes it when it breaks. We work on the technology and the service chain together, with the local bodies and communities who operate them.

Theme 04

Green and Inclusive Economy

Why this matters

India’s green transition is creating new work in clean mobility, renewable energy, sustainable agriculture and circular services. Whether that transition is inclusive depends on who can reach the work. Women, young people and small producers face the same barriers again and again: skills, finance and assets, market links and social norms. We work on those barriers together, so a new livelihood becomes a lasting one.

SHIELD: cities built to handle heat and water stress

Challenge

Growing cities face extreme heat and water stress, and the poorest households carry most of the exposure.

Approach

Nature-based solutions, decentralised water systems and community-managed assets, planned with municipal bodies and women-led self-help groups.

How the system worked, and why it matters

How the system worked

Women-led SHGs manage community assets. Municipal bodies bring the mandate and public systems. Nature-based and decentralised water technologies do the physical work, with climate and social outcomes measured from the start.

Why it matters

It treats resilience as city infrastructure that communities help run, an approach designed to scale from one city to many.

Mangroves as a climate buffer and a livelihood

Challenge

Coastal households live with storm and erosion risk, and few sources of steady income.

Approach

Mangrove restoration led by women’s collectives, working with the district administration and Forest Department, and linked to sustainable livelihoods.

How the system worked

Food Systems

Bargarh, Kandhamal, Dhenkanal and Rayagada, Odisha

2025–26

Measuring where the harvest is lost

Challenge

Tomato, strawberry and mango growers lose value between harvest and sale, and nobody had measured exactly where.

Approach

Structured loss assessment across four districts, using methods supported by Wageningen University, turning field evidence into practical solutions.

How the system worked, and why it matters

How the system worked

Field evidence from farmers and the chain is gathered with a tested methodology, so losses can be compared across crops and districts. Solutions are then judged on whether they are commercially viable for farmers, not only technically possible.

Why it matters

Starting with measurement means money and effort go to the points where value is actually lost, rather than where it is assumed to be.

Project Nirmal: sanitation for a whole town, not one toilet at a time

Challenge

Towns needed a sanitation system that works for the whole city, including what happens after the toilet: emptying, transport and treatment.

Approach

Demonstrated appropriate, sustainable sanitation service delivery in two municipalities, alongside solid waste facilities.

How the system worked, and why it matters

How the system worked

The service was built around the two municipalities, and the sanitation chain was treated as one system. Evidence from both towns was shaped to inform support frameworks at state and national level.

Why it matters

A demonstration built from day one to inform policy, so the model could travel well beyond two towns.

Swavalambi Stree: women in the driver’s seat of green mobility

Challenge

Women from low-income urban communities are kept out of well-paid mobility work by gender norms and a lack of access to vehicles and training.

Approach

Access to electric vehicles, skill building with driving schools, and ecosystem support so women can earn as independent e-auto drivers.

How the system worked, and why it matters

How the system worked

Women drivers at the centre. Driving schools and a local partner provide training. Vehicle access removes the biggest financial barrier. Electric vehicles make the livelihood part of the green transition.

Why it matters

It links the energy transition directly to women’s income, and challenges norms about who drives for a living.

A common yardstick for regenerative agriculture

Challenge

Regenerative practices are spreading, but their impact is measured in different ways, or not at all.

Approach

A monitoring, reporting and verification (MRV) framework with standardised methods, developed with IDH for the Regenerative Production Landscape Collaborative.

How the system worked, and why it matters

How the system worked

Developed in collaboration with IDH and partners, the framework sets what to measure and how, so results from farmers’ fields can be compared and verified across the landscape.

Why it matters

Practices only scale when their impact can be shown consistently. A shared framework is what makes that possible.

How we work

From a local challenge to a practical solution that lasts.

One continuous pathway, from diagnosis to wider adoption. The aim is not a bigger project. It is a better outcome that local institutions can carry forward.

  1. 01

    Understand

    Start with the people, institutions and markets involved, and the data on risk and bottlenecks.

    • Community and citizen insight
    • Service, livelihood and market diagnostics
    • Risk and bottleneck analysis
  2. 02

    Co-design

    Agree the outcome, the roles and the priorities with communities, partners and public institutions.

    • Shared outcome and roles
    • Practical priorities
    • Coordination arrangements
  3. 03

    Demonstrate

    Test the solution in real settings and gather evidence on adoption, inclusion and economics.

    • Field pilots and living labs
    • Technology adapted to context
    • Baselines and early results
  4. 04

    Deliver

    Run the programme with monitoring, learning and course-correction built in.

    • Programme management
    • Frontline skills, tools and routines
    • Adaptive management
  5. 05

    Strengthen

    Build the capability, financing and operating arrangements that let local institutions carry on.

    • Partner and institutional capability
    • Operating and financing models
    • Local ownership
  6. 06

    Scale and replicate

    Adapt what works for new places, and connect it to funding, partnership and enterprise pathways.

    • Adaptation across locations
    • Policy and practitioner learning
    • Funding and market pathways

ATechnology

Appropriate where it fits. Frontier where it helps.

We are not tied to one kind of technology. We choose by usefulness, context, maintainability and impact, never by novelty. Sometimes the right answer is a well-run community nursery. Sometimes it is sensors, remote sensing or digital early warning. Often it is a mix.

  • Useful solves the problem people actually have
  • Fits the context works with local skills, supply and conditions
  • Maintainable can be run and repaired locally
  • Measurable its impact can be shown

BCoalitions

Shared outcome. Clear roles. A route to delivery.

Complex problems need several organisations moving together. We help convene and structure partnerships so that everyone knows the outcome they are working towards, what they are responsible for, and how the work actually gets delivered.

CCSR and responsible business

From commitment to community outcome.

Companies and foundations bring ambition and capital. We turn that into programmes that work on the ground: designed with communities, delivered in the field, and measured credibly. Our role is programme design, delivery, the community interface, technical implementation, monitoring and learning.

DEvidence, MEL and MRV

Build evidence into delivery, not around it.

We establish baselines, track progress, harvest outcomes and use learning to adjust delivery while programmes are running. For climate and environmental work, MRV adds the discipline needed to make outcomes transparent and verifiable.
